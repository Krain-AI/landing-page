## 1. Executive Summary

KRAIN is the infrastructure layer for the AI economy. The project began as a consumer-facing AI app portal and has grown into a stack that now spans three layers:

1. **The KRAIN Marketplace** — a live discovery, reputation, and quest platform with a community of more than 150,000 across X, Telegram, and Discord. This is the product layer users interact with today.
2. **The KRAIN Protocol** — an AI-native L2 blockchain (EVM-compatible optimistic rollup) designed so that autonomous agents can execute, transact, and be priced as first-class economic actors.
3. **The $KRAIN token** — a fixed-supply utility and governance token whose value accrues from three on-chain revenue streams: sequencer margin on inference transactions, a 0.05% agent tax on x402 micropayments, and a 1% protocol fee on Initial Agent Offering (IAO) bonding curves.

Most AI x crypto projects sit on one side of a divide. Infrastructure-only projects raise large rounds but ship no product. Token-first projects accumulate market cap with no end-user surface. KRAIN combines a live product with the underlying agent execution layer that product needs as it scales.

**Traction.** The marketplace is live today with an engaged community of 150,000+ across X, Telegram, and Discord, an active quest and review system, and ambassador and creator programs running. (We deliberately lead with engagement rather than raw registration counts, which over-state real activity.)

**Funding.** KRAIN is bootstrapped to date and is currently raising a pre-seed round. Fundraising updates are shared through official channels.

This paper specifies the architecture, the token, and the path from today's marketplace to a fully on-chain agent economy. **Value is made, not allocated** — the economics in §5 are designed so that real usage, not a headline number, is what gives $KRAIN its worth.

## 2. The Problem

The AI ecosystem in 2026 has three structural gaps that the open internet alone cannot close.

**Discovery and trust are fragmented.** New AI apps and agents are launched daily. Quality, performance, and safety are difficult to compare. There is no neutral, structured directory with verified review data, performance metrics, and an incentive system that rewards honest curation rather than promotion budget.

**AI inference is opaque.** When a user calls a hosted model, they cannot verify that the model executed the prompt they sent, that the output was generated by the model they paid for, or that compute was billed honestly. This is acceptable for casual use. It is unacceptable for high-value agent workflows — financial agents, medical workflows, autonomous trading, governance decisions — that depend on auditable execution.

**Agents have no native economic layer.** Today's agents call APIs, settle payment in fiat through aggregators, and cannot easily own assets, transact directly with other agents, or be tokenized in a way that aligns incentives between agent developers and end users. The plumbing for an agent economy exists in fragments — wallets, identity standards, payment protocols — but no single chain has assembled them into a coherent execution environment.

KRAIN addresses all three. The marketplace solves discovery and trust at the application layer. The protocol solves verifiable inference and agent economics at the infrastructure layer.

## 3. The KRAIN Marketplace

The KRAIN Marketplace is the live product layer of the ecosystem. It is built around three pillars.

**Structured discovery.** AI apps and agents are listed in a searchable directory categorized by use case, model family, and performance. Discovery is natural-language-first: users can search in plain language — "agents for automating invoicing," "crypto compliance bots" — and receive personalized recommendations based on use case, model family, and prior activity, rather than navigating rigid category trees alone. Every listing supports verified reviews, structured performance data, and quest tasks that route users into hands-on evaluation rather than passive browsing.

**Reputation and rewards.** Users earn quest points for verified reviews, daily streaks, referrals, governance participation, and platform engagement. The points system has been operational since launch and currently underpins the airdrop allocation framework. Quest design favors signal over volume — low-effort engagement is capped, and review quality is weighted by accuracy and depth. Community signals — verified reviews and upvotes — feed a ranking that surfaces the highest-quality agents, so the best tools rise on merit rather than promotion budget. (Eligibility and anti-farming detail is in §5.2.)

**Ambassador and creator programs.** A multi-tier ambassador track recognizes long-term contributors. Creator programs route ecosystem grant funds to high-quality reviewers, content producers, and community moderators. Earned community roles (such as Ambassador, OG, and Kartel roles) are recognized within the contributor framework described in §5.2.

The marketplace today aggregates user demand. Once the protocol is live, the same user base becomes the natural entry point into the on-chain agent economy: each listed agent can graduate from a hosted listing into a fully tokenized, on-chain Smart Agent without the user having to learn a separate brand or interface.

## 4. The KRAIN Protocol

The KRAIN Protocol is an AI-native L2 blockchain. **In plain terms: it is a Layer-2 chain (it settles to Ethereum for security and lower cost) whose execution environment is purpose-built for AI — it can run and verify machine-learning work directly, instead of only recording the results of AI that ran somewhere else.** It is the substrate that lets agents execute verifiably, transact natively, and be priced as economic actors.

### 4.1 Architecture overview

KRAIN Protocol is an EVM-compatible optimistic rollup with a dual virtual machine:

- **EVM** for Solidity contracts: tokens, DeFi primitives, factories, bridges.
- **WASM (Rust-based)** for Smart Agents and the AI Virtual Machine (AIVM), giving agent code direct access to tensor primitives.

The chain settles to Ethereum L1 and uses a separate data availability layer for inference execution traces. The specific L1 partner and DA configuration will be selected based on cost, security, and ecosystem alignment.

```
            +----------------------+
            |     Ethereum L1      |   <- settlement
            +----------+-----------+
                       |
            +----------+-----------+
            |     KRAIN L2         |
            |  EVM + WASM (Stylus) |
            |  AIVM precompiles    |
            |  opML bisection      |
            +----------+-----------+
                       |
            +----------+-----------+
            |  Data Availability   |   <- execution traces
            +----------------------+
```

### 4.2 The AI Virtual Machine (AIVM)

The AIVM is a set of tensor operations exposed to the WASM runtime as host functions. Smart Agents call them directly, the same way a normal smart contract calls `keccak256` or `ecrecover`.

The first set of precompiles includes:

- `matmul` — dense matrix multiplication
- `relu`, `sigmoid`, `tanh`, `softmax` — activation functions
- `conv2d` — convolutional inference for vision workloads
- `attention` — scaled dot-product attention block

Tensor precompiles are the difference between an L2 that "supports AI" by hosting GPU billing wrappers and one that lets AI execution be expressed in contract code with deterministic gas costs.

### 4.3 Smart Agents

A Smart Agent is the on-chain representation of an autonomous AI agent. Each agent has:

- **A contract** (Rust/WASM via Stylus) that defines its behavior.
- **A wallet** (ERC-6551 Token Bound Account) that lets the agent hold assets, sign transactions, and pay other agents.
- **An identity** (compatible with ERC-8004 trustless agent identity standards) that supports on-chain reputation.
- **Optional content provenance** (ERC-7007) so outputs produced by the agent can be cryptographically attributed.

The AgentFactory contract lets a developer deploy a new Smart Agent in a single transaction. Templates cover common patterns: research agent, trading agent, customer-support agent, content agent, data-pipeline agent.

### 4.4 Optimistic Machine Learning (opML)

Running heavy ML inference on-chain is impractical today. KRAIN uses an optimistic verification model. **This is in active development and runs on the KRAIN devnet — it is not yet a production mainnet system.** The design works as follows:

1. A **solver node** runs the inference off-chain on real hardware (GPU) and posts the result on-chain together with a bond and a hash of the execution trace.
2. The full execution trace is published to the DA layer.
3. A configurable **challenge window** (default: two hours for high-value calls, instant for low-value calls) allows any watcher node to dispute the result.
4. Disputes are resolved via a **bisection game**: the chain narrows the disagreement to a single instruction step and adjudicates that step on-chain. The losing side forfeits its bond; the winning side is rewarded.

Honest solvers always win. Fraudulent solvers are slashed. This is the same model that secures fraud-proof rollups today, adapted to neural-network inference. The approach is grounded in published opML research and matches the verification philosophy of optimistic rollups already securing tens of billions in TVL.

### 4.5 The IAO Factory (Initial Agent Offering)

The IAO Factory is a permissionless launchpad for tokenized AI agents.

A developer deploys a Smart Agent and an Agent Token in one transaction. The Agent Token is sold against ETH or USDC on a **bonding curve**. *(A bonding curve is a smart contract that sets the token's price by formula as supply is bought or sold — early buys are cheaper, later buys cost more — so price discovery is automatic and rule-based, with no privileged early access and no separate exchange needed.)*

When the bonding curve reaches its graduation threshold (default: ~80% of supply sold, targeting roughly $500K market cap), the contract automatically:

1. Halts further bonding-curve trades.
2. Seeds a Uniswap V3 pool with the remaining tokens and accumulated reserves.
3. Locks the LP NFT in the factory contract so liquidity cannot be withdrawn.

Protocol fee: 1% of trade value on every bonding-curve buy or sell, paid to the KRAIN treasury. Developer fee: 1% of trade value, paid to the agent's creator wallet.

Agents that earn real revenue post-graduation can opt in to a fee distributor contract that streams a share of inference earnings back to Agent Token holders. This converts an agent token from pure speculation into a revenue-sharing instrument when the underlying agent is productive.

### 4.6 The x402 Payment Gateway

x402 is the open standard for HTTP-native micropayments: when an agent requests a resource, the responding server can reply with HTTP 402 and a payment instruction. KRAIN routes x402 payments through a settlement contract that takes a **0.05% protocol fee** (the "agent tax") on each transfer. The remaining 99.95% reaches the recipient agent.

Batch settlement reduces per-transaction gas cost so that micropayments down to fractions of a cent remain economically viable. The fee rate is set deliberately below typical payment processor margins so that agent-to-agent commerce is not bottlenecked by KRAIN's economics.

### 4.7 The Inference Router

The Inference Router is the facade that routes an inference request to the right solver. It considers cost, latency, model availability, solver reputation, and slashing history. Solvers register, stake, and earn reputation over time; persistently dishonest solvers lose bond and reputation.

This is the same role a load balancer plays in a Web2 cloud — except that solver performance and honesty are observable on-chain.

## 5. The $KRAIN Token

The $KRAIN token is the single native economic asset of the KRAIN ecosystem. It is a utility and governance token, not a gas token (gas on KRAIN L2 is paid in ETH, consistent with standard optimistic rollup design).

**What drives demand for $KRAIN.** Beyond staking and governance, sustained demand comes from real protocol activity: agents and users spend on supported apps and services; premium marketplace features and listing promotion are payable in $KRAIN; the IAO launchpad charges a fee in protocol terms; x402 routing and solver collateral consume the token's economic surface; and protocol revenue funds a buyback (§5.6). These are usage-driven, not narrative-driven. This is the core idea of the token design — **value is made through use, not assigned by a number.**

**Discretion.** This programme is wholly discretionary. Whether, when, to whom, and in what amount any allocation is made remains at the project's sole and absolute discretion. $KRAIN is a utility token: allocations under this programme carry no guaranteed monetary value, are not redeemable for fiat, and are not compensation denominated in any currency. Tier assignments and verified months for each recognised contributor are determined at the project's sole discretion.

### 5.3 Vesting

> **Plain-language summary for the community airdrop:** community airdrop recipients receive **25% of their $KRAIN at TGE**, and the remaining **75% vests linearly over 6 months**. Yes — the community airdrop vests; this is stated up front so there are no surprises at TGE.

- **Community airdrop (80M):** 25% at TGE, 75% linear over 6 months. **Custody is by audited smart contract** that releases vested tokens programmatically without further team approval — not a team-held wallet. Vesting can pause for sustained inactivity (see below) and resumes when the participant returns.
  - **What counts as "active":** ordinary app sign-in / interaction (using the platform). Activity is **not** defined as paying gas fees or grinding artificial tasks.
  - **Inactivity window:** vesting pauses only after **60–90 days** of inactivity (widened from an earlier 30-day proposal in response to community feedback).
  - **No claim-slashing:** there is no additional slashing penalty layered on top of vesting.
  - **Unclaimed tokens:** allocations left unclaimed after the claim window are directed to a **Season 2 community loyalty pool or burned** — they are not quietly returned to a general fund. The exact treatment will be confirmed ahead of TGE.
  - **Vesting has upside, not just a lock:** vested allocations carry governance weight and are eligible for staking-style fee participation, so holders are not merely waiting (this addresses the "what's the benefit of vesting?" question directly).
- **Team and founders (180M): milestone-based release, not a calendar vest.** Team and founder tokens are released only as the protocol hits real, verifiable milestones — aligning the team's upside with shipping a working network and with sustained protocol usage, rather than with the passage of time. This reflects feedback from KRAIN's market-maker partner that team release is best optimized to milestones rather than time-vesting.
  - **No team release before mainnet.** A hard floor: **zero** team or founder tokens unlock until the chain is live on mainnet. The team carries delivery risk first.
  - **Milestone-gated tranches.** After the mainnet floor, the remaining team allocation releases in tranches, each unlocked by a defined protocol milestone tied to the launch sequence (§6) and to concrete protocol traction. A sensible default structure — **the exact milestone→percentage split will be confirmed by the project ahead of mainnet:**
    - Mainnet live (production chain operational) — first tranche unlocks.
    - TGE / distribution and $KRAIN spendable on supported apps — next tranche.
    - Protocol traction: a target number of supported apps integrated and actively spending $KRAIN — next tranche.
    - Sustained protocol-revenue threshold maintained over a defined window (real fee revenue routed through §5.6) — next tranche.
    - Primary exchange listing (conditions-gated, §5.9) — final tranche.
  - **Time as a floor, not a trigger.** Where a milestone could in principle be gamed by pulling it forward, a minimum-elapsed-time floor may also apply so milestones can only *delay*, never *accelerate*, release. Total allocation is unchanged at **18% / 180M**.
- **Investors (150M):** Pre-seed has a 12-month cliff with a 25% unlock at month 13 and a 24-month linear tail. Future seed rounds use a shorter cliff with a 24-month tail.
- **Ecosystem and developer fund (300M):** **40M is released monthly-linear (~6.7M/month) over the first six months** for grants and hackathons. The remaining 260M is released quarterly by DAO vote over approximately three years.
- **Treasury (250M):** No automatic unlock. Deployed only by DAO vote.

### 5.4 Airdrop seasons

- **Season 1 (pre-TGE):** combines all existing phases — Origin, Alpha/Intract, and Catalyst (plus testnet participation) — into one TGE allocation. Earlier phases are weighted more, but all phases count. The explicit weights and the Season 1 / Season 2 split will be published ahead of the snapshot.
- **Season 2+ (post-TGE):** new seasons reward governance, building, staking, and continued network usage, in the multi-season model used by leading L2s.

### 5.5 Revenue streams

KRAIN captures revenue from three independent on-chain activities:

1. **Sequencer margin** on every transaction processed by the L2, with a target margin in the 15–25% range over L1 data-posting cost. The protocol takes 40% of sequencer revenue after operating costs; the sequencer operator keeps 60%.
2. **Agent tax** of 0.05% on every x402 micropayment settled through the gateway.
3. **IAO launchpad fee** of 1% on every bonding-curve trade.

These are independent: agent tax revenue does not depend on bonding-curve activity, sequencer revenue does not depend on agent-to-agent payments, and so on. The token's value accrual is therefore robust to which mode of agent activity dominates first.

### 5.6 Revenue routing and the buyback flywheel

All protocol revenue flows through a fixed split:

```
Protocol revenue
        |
        +-- 20% --> Staker reward pool        (paid in ETH, weekly)
        |
        +-- 40% --> $KRAIN buyback & burn     (deflationary)
        |
        +-- 40% --> Protocol treasury         (DAO-governed)
```

The 40% buyback creates a mechanical link between compute throughput and circulating supply: more inference, more agent payments, more launches → more revenue → more buybacks → less circulating $KRAIN. The 20% staker pool gives long-term holders real ETH yield from real fees, not inflationary token emissions. (Note: the buyback's effect on market price applies once a market exists — see §5.9 on TGE vs. listing.) This routing is modeled on revenue-routing proposals already adopted or under active discussion across major L2s.

### 5.7 Staking

$KRAIN holders can stake on KRAIN L2 to earn a share of protocol fees.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Reward source | Real protocol revenue (sequencer fees, agent tax, IAO fees) |
| Staker pool share | 20% of protocol revenue |
| Reward token | ETH |
| Distribution | Weekly epoch snapshots |
| Unstaking cooldown | 7 days |
| Governance weight | 1 staked $KRAIN = 1.5 votes; 1 unstaked = 1.0 vote |

Rewards are paid in ETH because that is the currency in which the underlying protocol fees are collected. This preserves the fixed supply of $KRAIN and avoids the dilution dynamics common to inflation-funded staking models.

$KRAIN holders earn boosted quest points on the marketplace. Tier thresholds and multipliers reward sustained holding:

### 5.9 From TGE to listing: utility first

KRAIN follows a **utility-first sequence**, and it is important to distinguish two separate events:

- **TGE (Token Generation Event):** $KRAIN is generated and distributed, and becomes **spendable for use on supported apps and services**. At TGE **there is no exchange market and no market price** — the token's first role is utility, not trading. Think of it like an in-ecosystem credit that real usage gives meaning to.
- **Listing event (later, conditions-gated):** once utility-driven demand has built, the market-maker seeds liquidity and a primary listing happens — **but only when conditions support opening at a fair value justified by proven demand**. There is no committed listing date; listing follows utility, not a calendar.

This is what we mean by **value is made, not allocated**. Rather than promising a price at launch, the design builds real usage first — spending on supported apps, agent payments, IAO activity, staking yield from real fees, and the buyback flywheel — and lets the market discover value from that usage. The float, liquidity, and any price discussion attach to the listing event, not to TGE.

### 5.10 Anti-dump design

Sustained value depends on managing post-listing sell pressure. KRAIN combines several mechanisms:

- **Activity-aligned vesting.** The community airdrop vests 25% at TGE, 75% over six months, in an audited smart contract, with the inactivity and unclaimed-token terms described in §5.3.
- **Milestone-gated team release.** Team and founder tokens unlock only as the protocol hits real milestones, with no team release before mainnet (§5.3) — the team's supply enters circulation in step with delivered network progress, not on a fixed calendar.
- **Staking yield as opportunity cost.** Real ETH yield on staked $KRAIN gives long-term holders a yield floor independent of price.
- **Two-season structure.** Season 2 rewards continued participation, governance, and staking.
- **Quest multipliers.** Holders earn more points; tier benefits reward holding.
- **Utility-first launch.** Because TGE precedes any market (§5.9), there is no day-one trading event to dump into; demand is built before liquidity is seeded.
- **Bridge rate limits.** When bridging opens, initial L2→L1 bridge limits cap per-address daily withdrawals (500K $KRAIN/day in week one, 2M $KRAIN/day in weeks two through four, unlimited after day 30) with a 1% bridge fee in the first 30 days that flows into the buyback contract.
- **Exchange-listing allocation is separate.** Community contributor allocation is **not pooled with, nor competed against, any exchange-listing allocation** — listing-program participants do not reduce the community's share.

## 6. Roadmap

KRAIN is shipping in phases. The current phase is foundation and pre-mainnet engineering. The timelines below are **rough targets, not commitments** — directional guidance to show the intended pace.

### Where the protocol stands (September 2026)

- **Devnet built.** The KRAIN devnet has been stood up on our own GPU hardware; it is where protocol features are proven before they reach a public network.
- **Verifiable inference demonstrated.** The opML fraud-proof flow has been demonstrated end to end across two independent hardware vendors (NVIDIA and AMD): a solver posts an inference result, a watcher re-executes it, and a wrong answer can be challenged.
- **On-chain inference demo.** An MNIST model is wired through the full devnet cycle (transaction in, inference, result on-chain); a recorded walkthrough is next.
- **Contract suite built.** The protocol's Stylus contracts build on a current toolchain; deployment of the suite to the Arbitrum Sepolia test network is the next step toward public testnet.

### Launch sequence (the order of events)

```
Testnet  ->  Mainnet  ->  Snapshot  ->  TGE  ->  Listing
```

Each milestone must complete before the next begins:
1. **Testnet** — public testing of the protocol.
